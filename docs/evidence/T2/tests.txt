## 3  the grid criterion

The roadmap's exit criterion reads "MC price within three standard errors of
closed form across the golden grid". It cannot be tested the way it reads, and
the reason is arithmetic rather than a difficulty with the code: three standard
errors is the bound for **one** comparison, 99.73% of which fall inside it, so
7,234 of them expect about twenty outside. A suite demanding none would be red
on a correct library and green only on a seed someone went looking for.

So each row is standardised — (Monte Carlo minus closed form) over the standard
error — and the sample of z-scores is held to being standard normal. That is
strictly stronger than the per-row bound: an estimator biased by a tenth of a
standard error passes every individual three-sigma test on this grid and fails
the test of the mean by eight sigma. It was measured: a deliberate +0.10 SE bias
is caught (grid mean 0.0768 against a bound of 0.0629), +0.05 and +0.03 are not.

Every row of the grid gets a test. Which one is decided before a path is drawn,
by the expected number of paths finishing in the money, `n N(w d2)`:

| rows | expected in the money | what is asserted |
|---|---|---|
| 6,147 | at least 450 | the z battery below |
| 323 | at least 450 once re-run at 1,048,576 paths | the same battery, separately |
| 764 | fewer than that at either count | the hit-count battery only; 630 of them saw no path in the money and are asserted to be priced at exactly zero |
| 0 | no randomness at all | exact comparison — this grid has no such row, and the branch is exercised by its own test case instead |

**The cut-off is 450 and it is derived.** A vanilla's discounted payoff is a
mixture: an atom at zero carrying 1 − p and a positive part above it. Writing
X = B·Y with B Bernoulli(p), the skewness for small p is about
(mu3/mu2^{3/2})/sqrt(p), and the first factor belongs to the payoff conditional
on finishing in the money — near-exponential in the tail of this grid, so about
6/2^{3/2} = 2.12. Cochran's condition for the central limit theorem to have
arrived, |skew|/sqrt(n) below 0.1, is then n·p >= (2.12/0.1)^2 = 450. Measured
independently at 20,000 repetitions per point, the mean z of a studentised
estimator is −0.073 at n·p = 200, −0.047 at 400, −0.026 at 1,000 and −0.011 at
4,000: the 0.05 level arrives near 400.

**Expected, not observed**, and this is a defect the review caught. The observed
count is correlated with the price estimate — the same paths make both — so
admitting a row on the strength of it selects on the statistic under test.
Measured on one row with an expected count of exactly 100, over 20,000
independent runs: the mean z of all runs is −0.11; the mean z of the runs
clearing an observed cut of 100 is **+0.45**. At the 450 cut-off the aggregate
effect is small (grid mean −0.0243 observed against −0.0232 expected), but the
principle is not, and the fix was one identifier.

Four assertions per battery, each at five sigma of its own sampling
distribution rather than three. Five, because these bounds are the noise on a
statistic, not on a measurement: a real defect arrives at tens of sigma — a
standard error understated by a factor of two puts the sample sd at 2.0, which
on 6,147 rows is 120 sigma — while a spurious failure at five sigma is a
one-in-three-million event on a run that is deterministic anyway. Three would
trade none of the power for a real chance of a red run on correct code.

**What the mean leg does not separate.** A studentised mean of a right-skewed
payoff has E[z] of about −c/sqrt(np) even when the estimator is exact, so a
little of the budget is spent before any defect exists. Measured across five
disjoint seed families the grid mean sits at −0.009 with a spread of 0.006,
against a bound of 0.063: about a seventh. That is the price of a cut-off at 450
rather than at four thousand, and it is stated in the test rather than left to
be discovered.

**The 764 rows Monte Carlo cannot price** are the ones `golden/SCHEMA.md`
already calls numerically dead — 3.8% of the grid is below 1e-15 — and the
reason they get no price assertion is worth naming: the estimate and its error
bar collapse together. A handful of sampled payoffs from a heavy right tail
produce a small mean *and* a small sample spread, so the z-score is large and
means nothing; the worst on this run is 440.8, on a deep out-of-the-money put.
What does cover them is the hit-count battery: the paths land in the money at
the rate the closed form says, on 5,752 standardised rows and on both Poisson
tails, which is the part of the claim Monte Carlo can still speak to.

## 4  Euler's two rates

Two errors, not one, and a suite measuring either alone would pass on an
implementation that had the other badly wrong. Both are claims the site will
make — D4 shows the path picture, D6 uses the price — so both are tested (I1).

**Strong, order one half.** The exact sampler has no discretisation error, so
driving both schemes from the same Brownian increment isolates Euler's mistake
exactly: the difference *is* the error, with no sampling noise in front of it.

```
    steps        dt      mean error        SE     ratio   fitted
        1   1.000000      4.396988   0.036459         -       no
        2   0.500000      3.376613   0.023409     1.302       no
        4   0.250000      2.513907   0.016537     1.343       no
        8   0.125000      1.818615   0.011623     1.382      yes
       16   0.062500      1.284463   0.007765     1.416      yes
       32   0.031250      0.921620   0.005620     1.394      yes
       64   0.015625      0.655078   0.003962     1.407      yes
      128   0.007812      0.466709   0.002792     1.404      yes
      256   0.003906      0.326473   0.001950     1.430      yes
    slope over dt < 0.13: 0.4932 +/- 0.0021 (0.5 expected), worst residual 0.0084
```

Half, not one: the term Euler drops is (sigma^2/2)(Z^2 − 1) S dt per step, mean
zero, so over T/dt steps the omissions add like a random walk to
sqrt(T/dt)·dt = sqrt(T·dt). Milstein's correction is exactly that term. The
ratios climb toward sqrt(2) = 1.414 and settle; the coarse levels are reported
and not fitted, because at half a year a step Euler is not approximating
anything yet and a fit across the whole range would measure the climb.

**Weak, order one.** The same paths through a payoff and a discount factor, with
common random numbers so the difference carries a fraction of the noise either
estimate has alone.

```
    steps        dt          bias         SE   bias/SE   bias/dt
        2   0.500000     0.036115   0.003638       9.9    0.0722
        4   0.250000     0.050836   0.002617      19.4    0.2033
        8   0.125000     0.033433   0.001887      17.7    0.2675
       16   0.062500     0.017557   0.001353      13.0    0.2809
       32   0.031250     0.008636   0.000965       9.0    0.2764
       64   0.015625     0.005505   0.000682       8.1    0.3523
    slope over dt < 0.13: 0.8831 +/- 0.0621 (1.0 expected)
```

The `bias/dt` column is the evidence that the fit is taken where the rate has
arrived: 0.07 at half a year a step, climbing to about a third and staying
there. Fitted across all six levels the slope would be 0.63, and the mistake
would be the fit's rather than the scheme's.

The bound on each slope is the measurement's own uncertainty five times over,
propagated from the standard error each point carries, plus a stated allowance
for the curvature a finite dt still has. Every level's bias is separately
required to be resolved above six of its own standard errors, so what is fitted
is a measurement rather than noise.

And the practical statement, made once with the engine rather than the pieces:
at 512 steps and 400,000 antithetic paths the Euler price is 13.04013 +/-
0.02717 against a closed form of 13.020281, with an extrapolated bias of 6.5e-4.

## 5  what each suite would catch

**rng** — that the browser and the library have stopped drawing the same
numbers. The fixture's uniforms are asserted bit for bit, not to a tolerance: a
uniform is an integer expression times a power of two, so two correct
implementations return the same double or one of them is wrong, and a swapped
high and low word or an off-by-one shift is invisible at 1e-12. The normals are
held to `docs/rng.md`'s 1e-12 and in fact agree bit for bit as well, all thirty
of them. Also: that AS241 has been mistyped (12 mpmath reference values in all
three branches, at 8 eps relative, plus a round-trip through erfc over 1,199
values of p from 1e-300 to 0.5); that the seed expansion can reach the all-zero
state (fmix32 inverted and round-tripped over 2^20 inputs, and the state checked
over 2^20 seeds); that a sweep's rows are not independent (no two of 8,000 seeds
within 341,411 of each other, worst correlation 0.0506 over 2,016 stream pairs
against a computed bound of 0.0972).

**monte-carlo** — that the estimator is biased, that its standard error is
wrong, that the antithetic standard error is computed from paths rather than
pairs, that the pathwise delta has lost its indicator or its sign for puts, that
the stream is consumed at the wrong rate, that the discount is applied twice,
that an antithetic partner draws fresh normals, that a NaN can escape from an
input the closed form accepts. The standard error is pinned to roughly
−5%/+3% by the grid battery's spread leg; the dedicated 400-run spread test is
coarser at 17.7% and is there for what the grid cannot see, which is the whole
estimator end to end.

**euler** — that the discretisation converges at the wrong rate, in either of
the two senses, or that the sign of the drift or the scaling of the diffusion is
wrong. Also that Euler has quietly stopped being wrong: the strong slope would
go to 1 if the Milstein term were added.

## 6  the adversarial review

A fresh session was given `docs/rng.md`, the sources and the tests and asked to
find errors rather than confirm the work. It re-derived the specification symbol
by symbol, checked all 48 AS241 coefficients character by character and measured
how tightly each is pinned by the suite (all to 6.5e-12 relative or better, 36
of them under 1e-14), transcribed Blackman and Vigna's reference implementation
independently and confirmed 100,000 draws agree for five seeds, and mutation-
tested. Two rounds, 69 mutations.

### what it found, and what happened

| # | finding | severity | outcome |
|---|---|---|---|
