## 3. The exit criteria, worked through
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The roadmap states three. Each is quoted, then read, then met with a number from
section 2.

### "three-way agreement (I20) within stated tolerances across the golden grid"

I20 is the constitution's: *"Closed form, Monte Carlo and finite differences MUST
agree within stated tolerances; analytic and bump Greeks likewise; Monte Carlo
standard error is reported, never hidden."*

**The tolerances, stated.** A finite-difference tolerance without a grid beside
it means nothing, so each sweep names its grid.

| Comparison | Grid | Scale | Worst | Tolerance |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| PDE vs closed form, price | 512 x 256, alpha 6, 7200 rows | max(\|ref\|, S) | 8.93e-05 | 2e-04 |
| PDE vs closed form, price | the same | max(\|ref\|, 1) — SCHEMA.md's rule | 1.28e-03 | 3e-03 |
| PDE vs closed form, delta | the same | max(\|ref\|, S) | 3.44e-06 | 1e-05 |
| PDE vs closed form, gamma | the same | max(\|ref\|, S) | 3.56e-07 | 1e-06 |
| PDE vs closed form, price | 1024 x 512, 136 rows | max(\|ref\|, S) | 2.16e-05 | 5e-05 |
| Monte Carlo vs closed form | 131,072 antithetic paths, 119 rows | standard errors | worst \|z\| 3.17 | bound 4.93 |

**Why two scales for the price.** `golden/SCHEMA.md`'s rule — relative above 1.0,
absolute below — is right for the closed form, whose noise floor is a few ulps of
the spot. It is the wrong rule for a grid: a discretisation error is a fraction of
the value function's curvature, and a European vanilla's only curvature scale is
the underlying's, so the same grid that prices a hundred-unit option to 1e-5
prices a 1e-19 option to 1e-5 too. Dividing by the option's own price there
measures how far out of the money it is. Both scales are reported and both are
asserted; neither is allowed to hide behind the other.

**The Monte Carlo leg is not a per-row three-standard-error rule**, for the reason
T2 established and this pack does not restate: on a sample of this size roughly
one row in three hundred exceeds three standard errors of a correct estimator.
What is asserted is the sample's shape — mean 0 to within 4/sqrt(n), spread
between 0.6 and 1.5, and no row beyond the quantile a sample of this size should
reach. Measured: mean -0.218 (expected 0 +/- 0.092), sd 0.977 (expected 1 +/-
0.065), one row beyond three (0.32 expected), worst |z| 3.167 against a bound of
4.926. Standard errors run from 5.0e-06 to 5.8e-02 of the price and are printed,
never hidden.

**The claim about the two numerical methods against each other** follows: each is
within its own tolerance of the same closed form, so they are within the sum.
Comparing them directly would be a weaker statement, not a stronger one — two
methods can agree with each other and both be wrong about the model.

**Underneath the numbers, four measurements that make the scheme's claims claims
rather than hopes**, each in `test_pde.cpp`:

  - the error falls as `dx^2`: ratios 3.97, 3.99, 4.00 over three doublings;
  - the error falls as `dtau^2`: ratios 4.31, 4.99, 3.51 over three doublings,
    with the space error subtracted first;
  - the truncated domain contributes nothing measurable from three total
    volatilities outward — 1.3e-08 across alpha in [3, 12] at a fixed `dx`,
    against a discretisation error of 2.8e-06 beside it, and 3.2e-04 at alpha 2,
    which is what makes it a measurement of the boundary rather than of nothing;
  - the Crank-Nicolson matrix is strictly diagonally dominant with a margin of
    1.000 on every row of the grid, and the mesh Peclet number reaches 0.102
    against the bound of 2 where the scheme stops being an M-matrix.

### "bump Greeks match analytic within tolerance"

Over all 7,200 rows of the main grid, at the default bump sizes:

| Greek | Worst scaled error | Tolerance |
|---|---|---|
| price | 0 (it is the pricer's own answer, passed through) | 0 |
| delta | 2.81e-10 | 1e-09 |
| gamma | 2.01e-08 | 1e-07 |
| vega | 1.20e-08 | 1e-07 |
| theta | 2.55e-09 | 1e-08 |
| rho | 2.45e-09 | 1e-08 |
| dividend_rho | 2.77e-09 | 1e-08 |

The spread between delta and gamma is the analysis, measured: a first difference
reaches about `eps^(2/3)` and a second only `eps^(1/2)`. The defaults are not the
theory's numbers but the measured optima — each bump swept over four decades
against the analytic Greek, and set where the worst error over the grid was
smallest. `test_bump_greeks.cpp` re-runs a coarser version of that sweep and
asserts that the minimum is at the default and that the error rises on *both*
sides of it, which is the half of the story a one-sided test would miss.

### "implied vol round-trips golden prices to 1e-8"

The sentence has two readings and both are tested, because only one is
satisfiable everywhere.

**The price round trip** — price to volatility to price — is well posed on every
row where a solution exists. Over all 7,234 rows of the main grid and the edge
block: **worst scaled residual 1.48e-12**, four orders inside the criterion. None
refused, none unconverged, worst iteration count 27.

**The volatility round trip** — comparing the recovered volatility with the one
the file was generated at — is well posed only where the price depends on the
volatility at all. On 766 of the 7,200 main-grid rows vega is at or below 1e-06,
and on the worst of them a whole volatility point moves the price by less than one
unit in its last place. Over the 6,434 rows where vega exceeds 1e-06 the worst
error is **3.32e-07**; over all rows it is 1.50e-01, on a put 24 standard
deviations in the money.

Rather than assert a figure the arithmetic cannot deliver, the test computes what
each row's price affords — the tolerance the solver stopped at, divided by the
*smaller* of the two vegas, since vega is unimodal in the volatility and the
smallest slope over an interval is at one of its ends — and requires the error to
be inside it. Measured: **worst 1.00 times what the price affords**, asserted at
2, the factor the linearisation needs. That is a stronger statement than any fixed
tolerance, because it holds on the dead rows too.

**The fallback is exercised deliberately.** Newton finishes all 6,910 iterating
rows on its own, so Brent is a path nothing reaches. Giving Newton a budget of one
step routes 982 of 1,029 sampled rows through Brent instead; the worst scaled
reprice is then 1.07e-12, still four orders inside the criterion. Brent's own
budget is fixed inside the solver rather than shared with Newton's, precisely so
that this is a test of the fallback and not of an exhausted counter.

## 4. What each new suite would catch
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

`associations` — an input `require_valid` accepts and the closed form returns a
NaN for; a domain check whose association drifts from the formula it guards; a
repair that narrowed the domain and stopped pricing a row T2 relies on; a golden
row falling outside the accepted domain.

`tridiagonal` — a Thomas sweep that reads the wrong band, loses a pivot, or
mis-indexes the back substitution; a dominance margin that measures something
else; a projected sweep that substitutes away from the exercise region instead of
towards it, or that fails to satisfy complementarity; the one-sided precondition
being violated without anyone noticing.

`pde` — a spot that is not exactly on a node; a scheme that is not second order in
space or in time; a domain too narrow for its Dirichlet conditions; a payoff
projection that does not buy what it claims; a Rannacher start that does not damp;
a matrix an unpivoted sweep cannot be trusted with; and the golden grid itself,
priced.

`implied-vol` — Brent failing on a bracketed root, or taking more calls than
bisection would; a price that no volatility produces being answered rather than
refused; a solver that is not monotone in its input; a round trip outside 1e-8;
a volatility claimed more precisely than its price supports.

`bump-greeks` — a Greek differenced with the wrong sign or the wrong bump; a bump
size that is not the optimum; a difference quotient that has quietly become the
analytic formula; a bump that leaves the domain being taken anyway.

`american` — an American value below its European counterpart or below intrinsic;
the two exact identities broken; a projected sweep pointed the wrong way, which
costs an order of convergence and nothing else; the put-call bounds breached where
they hold; a grid that does not converge.

`three-way` — the three methods agreeing pairwise but not together; a Monte Carlo
whose standard error is not the standard error of what it reports; Greeks that
agree with the formula but not with each other.

## 5. The adversarial review: twenty-four mutations, and the three that survive
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

A green suite says the tests pass. It does not say the tests could fail. Every
number in this pack was produced by code that was then deliberately broken,
twenty-four ways, to find out which breakages the suite notices. Each mutation was
applied on its own, built with warnings as errors, and run whole at sweep scale 4;
the source was restored before the next.

**Caught — 21.**

| # | The mutation | Suites that fail |
|---|---|---|
| M1 | convection sign flipped in the PDE operator | american, bump-greeks, pde, three-way |
| M2 | theta = 1 always: backward Euler wearing Crank-Nicolson's name | american, bump-greeks, pde, three-way |
| M3 | discounting dropped from the PDE reaction term | american, bump-greeks, pde, three-way |
| M4 | payoff sampled at nodes instead of averaged over its cell | bump-greeks, pde |
| M5 | Rannacher start skipped: pure Crank-Nicolson from the payoff | pde |
| M6 | grid delta not converted out of log-space (missing 1/S) | pde, three-way |
| M7 | grid gamma missing the -u_x term of the change of variable | pde, three-way |
| M8 | American constraint applied at the boundaries only | american |
| M9 | projected sweep always sweeps Lower: wrong for a call | american |
| M9b | projected sweep always sweeps Upper: wrong for a put | american |
| M10 | Thomas reads the superdiagonal where the subdiagonal belongs | american, bump-greeks, pde, three-way, tridiagonal |
| M11 | projected sweep substitutes away from the exercise region | tridiagonal |
| M12 | theta's minus sign dropped in the bump Greeks | bump-greeks, three-way |
| M13 | gamma bumped with delta's step instead of its own | bump-greeks, three-way |
| M15 | both drift checks replaced by T1's single combined product | associations |
| M16 | half_variance_time regrouped as (0.5 sigma)(sigma T) | associations |
| M17 | Brent always interpolates: the bisection guard removed | implied-vol |
| M19 | the discount factor loses its minus sign | 33 cases across 11 files |
| M20 | grid domain no longer stretched to cover the forward log-price | pde |
| M22 | the implied-vol tolerance made absolute instead of scaled | implied-vol |
| M23 | antithetic partner drawn fresh instead of negated (a T2 check) | monte-carlo |

**M9 is the one worth reading twice.** Brennan and Schwartz's sweep must
substitute *towards* the exercise region, and a put's direction is the opposite of
a call's. Point one of them the wrong way and the answer is still **consistent**:
it converges to the same limit, so no comparison at a single resolution can see
it — the first version of this suite did not, and the mutation survived. What
changes is the *rate*, because at every step the constraint is applied to a node
whose neighbour has not been constrained yet and the free boundary lands a node
out. Measured, on the two rows the test now uses:

    row                       correct direction    reversed
    put  S=90 K=100           3.40 3.14 2.95       2.21 2.11 2.06
    call S=120 K=90 q=0.04    3.95 4.00 4.00       2.28 2.16 2.09

Four is second order and two is first. The test asserts the rate rather than the
value, which is the only thing a price can say about this mistake.

**Survived — 3. Each is a limit of resolution, not a defect, and each is named
here rather than left to be found.**

  - **M14 — implied vol returns its seed without iterating.** Not a defect: the
    Newton loop's only exit with an answer is its `return`, and every other exit
    falls through to Brent, so deleting Newton entirely leaves a correct solver
    and a slower one. Writing this mutation is what found a dead `hand_over` flag,
    which is now gone.

  - **M18 — American boundary values not raised to immediate exercise.** The
    boundary sits six total volatilities from the spot, the strike and the
    forward, so its treatment cannot reach the answer: at `alpha >= 1` the two
    versions agree to the last bit, and they first differ at `alpha = 0.5`
    (12.0091 against 12.0078), where the domain is far too narrow for either to be
    right. The line is kept because it is correct and because the boundary node's
    own value should satisfy the constraint; it is unfalsifiable by any price this
    library reports.

  - **M21 — Newton's stall guard removed.** Newton never stalls on the golden
    grid, so the guard is defensive code for inputs outside the test set. It costs
    nothing and it is not exercised; Brent still catches every other way Newton
    can fail.

**Three mutations did not compile**, each because removing the behaviour left a
variable or a function unused and `-Werror` refused it. They were rewritten to
keep the code compiling and are in the table above (M4, M8, M20).
